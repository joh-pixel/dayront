# How to Speed Up or Slow Down Audio Without Changing Pitch

Ever wanted to listen to a lecture at 1.5× speed without the chipmunk voices? Or slow down a guitar solo to learn each note without it sounding like a bass? That’s what **tempo scaling with pitch preservation** does – and Dayront’s Speed Changer makes it happen instantly, for free, in your browser.

## Why Pitch Matters

When you simply play audio faster (like speeding up a tape), the pitch rises. Voices become squeaky, music goes out of tune. True time‑stretching changes the tempo while keeping the pitch constant. This is done through advanced signal processing algorithms – and now you can do it online without installing anything.

### Common Use Cases

- **Podcast editing** – condense long‑winded segments without alienating listeners.
- **Language learning** – slow down native speakers to catch every word.
- **Music practice** – slow down a fast passage to learn it, then speed it up to test yourself.
- **Accessibility** – adjust audiobook speed to your comfort level.

## How the Speed Changer Works (Simplified)

Traditional playback speed changes alter the pitch. The **phase vocoder** technique, used by FFmpeg and Dayront, processes audio in short overlapping frames, adjusts the spacing between them, and then resynthesises the signal – all without affecting frequency.

Here’s a simple comparison:

| Speed Factor | Effect on Pitch (without preservation) | Dayront (preserved) |
|--------------|----------------------------------------|---------------------|
| 0.75×        | Lowers by ~5 semitones                 | Pitch unchanged     |
| 1.25×        | Raises by ~3 semitones                 | Pitch unchanged     |
| 2.0×         | Raises by 12 semitones (octave)        | Pitch unchanged     |

## Limitations and Tips

- Extreme settings (below 0.5× or above 2.0×) can introduce artifacts. For best quality, stay within 0.75× – 1.5×.
- Files with strong transients (like drums) may sound slightly blurred when slowed down.
- Always save a copy of your original file before experimenting.
