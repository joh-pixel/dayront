## Audiobook Technical Requirements

Before you start, understand what platforms expect:

| Platform | Format | Bitrate | Loudness | Notes |
|----------|--------|---------|----------|-------|
| **Audible/ACX** | MP3 | 192 kbps | -18 to -23 LUFS | Requires room tone at beginning/end |
| **Findaway Voices** | MP3 | 192 kbps | -16 to -20 LUFS | Accepts WAV too |
| **Apple Books** | M4A/MP3 | 192 kbps | -16 LUFS | Auto-converts |
| **Kobo Writing Life** | MP3 | 128 kbps | -16 LUFS | Simpler requirements |

**Key requirements:**
- Consistent loudness (typically -16 to -20 LUFS)
- Clear, noise-free narration
- Chapter breaks (separate files or merged with markers)
- Proper metadata (title, author, chapter)

## The Complete Audiobook Creation Workflow

### Step 1: Record Your Narration

Record each chapter separately. This makes editing easier and keeps files manageable.

**Recording tips:**
- Use a quiet room with soft surfaces
- Position mic 4–6 inches from mouth
- Keep consistent distance throughout
- Record 2–3 seconds of room tone before speaking
- Pause briefly between sections

**Why record in chapters?**
- Easier to re-record mistakes
- Simpler to organize
- Less processing time per file
- Cleaner final product

### Step 2: Clean Up Each Chapter

Apply the cleanup workflow to each chapter file:

**For each chapter:**

1. **Trim** – Remove silence and mistakes using the **[Audio Cutter](/tools/audio-cutter)**.

2. **Compress** – Apply level 2–3 compression using the **[Audio Compressor](/tools/audio-compressor)**. This evens out volume and reduces noise.

3. **Boost** – Add +6 to +9 dB using the **[Volume Booster](/tools/volume-booster)**. Target -16 to -20 LUFS.

4. **Convert** – Output as MP3 at 192 kbps using the **[Audio Converter](/tools/audio-converter)**.

5. **Clean metadata** – Toggle "Clean File Privacy Before Download."

### Step 3: Merge Chapters (Optional)

If you want a single audiobook file with all chapters:

1. Open the **[Audio Merger](/tools/audio-merger)**.
2. Upload all chapter files in order.
3. Merge.
4. Download.

**Pro tip:** Add 1–2 seconds of silence between chapters for a natural pause. You can create a short silent file and insert it between chapters in the merger.

### Step 4: Final Processing

Apply final polish to the complete audiobook:

1. **Light compression** – Level 2 to glue everything together.
2. **Final boost** – Ensure consistent loudness.
3. **Convert** – MP3 at 192 kbps (Audible standard) or 128 kbps (other platforms).
4. **Clean metadata** – Remove all identifying information.

### Step 5: Verify Quality

Listen to samples from different sections:
- Beginning of each chapter
- Middle sections
- Quiet passages
- Loud passages

Check for:
- Consistent volume
- No background noise
- Clear speech
- Natural pacing

## Real-World Example: Creating an Audiobook from a Blog Series

**The scenario:**
You have a 10-part blog series about productivity. Each post is about 2,000 words (roughly 15 minutes of narration). You want to create an audiobook.

**The workflow:**

**Step 1: Record each post as a chapter**
- 10 chapters, each ~15 minutes
- Total recording time: ~2.5 hours

**Step 2: Clean each chapter**
- Trim silence from each chapter (2 min each = 20 min total)
- Compress each chapter at level 2 (1 min each = 10 min total)
- Boost each chapter +6 dB (1 min each = 10 min total)

**Step 3: Merge chapters into one audiobook**
- Use Audio Merger to combine all 10 chapters (2 min)
- Result: A 2.5-hour audiobook file

**Step 4: Final processing**
- Light compression (1 min)
- Convert to MP3 at 192 kbps (2 min)
- Clean metadata (instant)

**Step 5: Publish**
- Upload to Findaway Voices or similar platform
- Set price and distribution

**Total processing time:** ~45 minutes
**Result:** A professional audiobook ready for sale.

## Privacy: Why Local Audiobook Creation Matters

When you use cloud-based audio tools for audiobook creation, your content is uploaded to third-party servers. This creates risks:

**1. Content Theft**
Your audiobook – potentially months of work – could be stolen if the service is breached.

**2. Voice Exposure**
Your voice is a biometric identifier. Uploading hours of narration exposes you to unknown parties.

**3. Metadata Leakage**
Audio files contain metadata about you, your device, and your location.

**4. Data Retention**
Many services keep copies of your files indefinitely.

**Dayront's browser tools eliminate these risks:**
- Files stay on your device
- Processing happens locally with WebAssembly
- "Clean File Privacy" strips metadata
- No accounts, no tracking

**Privacy checklist before creating an audiobook:**
- [ ] Process locally with Dayront tools
- [ ] Toggle "Clean File Privacy" on every download
- [ ] Avoid cloud tools for sensitive narration
- [ ] Store audiobook files securely
