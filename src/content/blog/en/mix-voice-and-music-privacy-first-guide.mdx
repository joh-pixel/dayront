## Why Mixing Voice and Music Matters

### The Clarity Problem

When music plays behind speech, two things compete for the listener's attention. If the music is too loud, speech becomes unintelligible. If the music is too quiet, it fails to add atmosphere or emotion.

Professional mixers solve this with **proper level balancing** and **ducking** (automatically lowering music when voice is present).

### The Professional Standard

Research and industry practice suggest:

| Element | Recommended Level | Relative to Voice |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Voice (speech) | -16 LUFS | Reference (0 dB) |
| Background music (during speech) | -26 to -30 LUFS | -10 to -14 dB below voice |
| Music (intro/outro, no speech) | -16 to -20 LUFS | Equal or slightly quieter |
| Sound effects | -20 to -24 LUFS | -4 to -8 dB below voice |

**Key insight:** During speech, music should be 10–14 dB quieter than the voice. When there's no speech, music can come up to full level.

## How to Mix Voice and Music (Step-by-Step)

### Step 1: Prepare Your Voice Track

Before mixing, optimize your voice recording:

1. **Trim** – Remove silences and unwanted sections using the **[Audio Cutter](/tools/audio-cutter)**.
2. **Compress** – Apply level 3–4 compression using the **[Audio Compressor](/tools/audio-compressor)** to even out volume.
3. **Boost** – Bring voice to approximately -16 LUFS using the **[Volume Booster](/tools/volume-booster)**. Add +6 to +9 dB as needed.

**Result:** A clean, consistent voice track ready for mixing.

### Step 2: Prepare Your Music Track

Now prepare the background music:

1. **Trim** – Cut music to the desired length using the Audio Cutter.
2. **Adjust volume** – Set music level relative to voice. For background music during speech, music should be 10–14 dB quieter. Use the Volume Booster carefully – if the music is already loud, you may need to start with a quieter source.

**Pro tip:** If your music is too loud even at its lowest setting, consider using a different track with a simpler arrangement (e.g., solo piano or acoustic guitar instead of full orchestration).

### Step 3: Merge Voice and Music

Combine the tracks using the Audio Merger:

1. Open the **[Audio Merger](/tools/audio-merger)**.
2. Upload the music file first (this becomes the background).
3. Upload the voice file second (this plays over the music).
4. Arrange order if needed (music first, then voice).
5. Merge and download.

**Result:** A mixed audio file with music under voice.

### Step 4: Apply Final Polish

After merging:

1. **Compress again** – Apply light compression (level 2–3) to glue the mix together.
2. **Boost if needed** – If the combined mix is too quiet, add +3 to +6 dB.
3. **Convert** – Output as MP3 at 128 kbps (podcast) or 192 kbps (video).

### Step 5: Clean Metadata

Toggle **"Clean File Privacy Before Download"** to remove identifying information.

## The Science of Ducking

### What Is Ducking?

Ducking is the automatic lowering of background music when voice is present. When the speaker pauses, the music comes back up. This creates a professional, dynamic mix where the voice is always clear.

### How to Achieve Ducking with Dayront Tools

While Dayront doesn't have a dedicated ducking tool yet, you can achieve a similar effect manually:

**Method 1: Volume Automation (Manual)**
1. Split your music into segments: quiet parts (under voice) and louder parts (between voice).
2. Process each segment separately with the Volume Booster.
3. Merge all segments back together.

**Method 2: Compression Sidechain (Coming Soon)**
Dayront is developing a proper ducking tool that will automatically lower music when voice is detected.

**For now:** The simplest approach is to keep background music at a consistently low level (-26 to -30 LUFS) throughout the speech sections. This avoids the need for ducking while maintaining clarity.

## Common Mixing Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Podcast Intro with Music

**The goal:** Music plays at full level for 5 seconds, then fades under voiceover.

**Workflow:**
1. Trim music to desired length.
2. Separate music into two parts: intro (full level) and background (low level).
3. Reduce the background portion's volume using Volume Booster (or choose a naturally quieter section).
4. Merge: Intro music → Voiceover + background music → Main content.

### Scenario 2: YouTube Video with Background Music

**The goal:** Subtle background music throughout the video without distracting from speech.

**Workflow:**
1. Prepare voice track (compress + boost).
2. Set music at -26 to -30 LUFS (10–14 dB below voice).
3. Merge voice and music.
4. Apply final compression.

### Scenario 3: Music Bed for Social Media Clip

**The goal:** Upbeat music under a short voiceover for TikTok or Instagram.

**Workflow:**
1. Keep voice clip short (15–30 seconds).
2. Choose energetic music.
3. Set music 10 dB below voice.
4. Merge and export.

## Privacy: Why Local Mixing Matters

When you use cloud-based audio mixers, your voice recordings and music tracks are uploaded to third-party servers. This creates risks:

**1. Voice Exposure**
Your voice is a unique biometric identifier. Uploading it to cloud tools means exposing it to unknown parties.

**2. Unreleased Content at Risk**
Podcast episodes, voiceovers, and music tracks that haven't been published are vulnerable to theft.

**3. Metadata Leakage**
Audio files contain metadata about you, your device, and your location. Cloud tools can access it.

**4. Data Retention**
Many services keep copies of your files indefinitely.
