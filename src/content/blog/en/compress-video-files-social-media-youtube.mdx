## Why Video Compression Matters

### The Size Problem

Modern cameras and phones record in 4K or even 8K. That's great for quality, but terrible for file size:

| Resolution | Bitrate | File Size (per minute) |
|------------|---------|------------------------|
| 4K (3840×2160) | 50 Mbps | ~375 MB |
| 1080p (1920×1080) | 12 Mbps | ~90 MB |
| 720p (1280×720) | 6 Mbps | ~45 MB |
| 480p (854×480) | 3 Mbps | ~22 MB |

A 10-minute 4K video is nearly 4 GB. That's too large for most platforms and painful to upload.

### Platform Limits

Each platform has different requirements:

| Platform | Max File Size | Recommended Resolution | Recommended Bitrate |
|----------|---------------|------------------------|---------------------|
| YouTube | 256 GB | 1080p or 4K | 8–12 Mbps (1080p) |
| TikTok | 287 MB (iOS) / 72 MB (Android) | 1080×1920 (vertical) | 4–6 Mbps |
| Instagram Reels | 4 GB (but keep small) | 1080×1920 (vertical) | 4–6 Mbps |
| Instagram Feed | 4 GB | 1080×1080 or 1080×1350 | 4–6 Mbps |
| LinkedIn | 5 GB | 1080p or 720p | 4–8 Mbps |
| Email/Messaging | 25 MB (typical limit) | 480p or 720p | 2–4 Mbps |

### The Upload Speed Factor

Even if a platform accepts large files, uploading them takes time. A 2 GB file on a 10 Mbps upload connection takes nearly 30 minutes. Compress it to 200 MB, and it takes 3 minutes.

**Bottom line:** Compression saves time, bandwidth, and frustration – when done correctly.

## Understanding Video Compression

### What Compression Does

Video compression reduces file size by:

1. **Removing redundant information** – Areas of the frame that don't change between frames
2. **Reducing resolution** – Fewer pixels = smaller file
3. **Lowering bitrate** – Less data per second = smaller file
4. **Using efficient codecs** – H.265 is more efficient than H.264

### Lossy vs. Lossless

All practical video compression is **lossy** – some information is permanently removed. The goal is to remove information that viewers won't notice.

**Key insight:** The human eye is more sensitive to changes in brightness than color, and more sensitive to detail in the center of vision than the edges. Good compression exploits these limitations.

## The Right Settings for Every Platform

### YouTube Compression Settings

YouTube re-compresses your video after upload, so you want to upload a file that's high enough quality to survive that re-compression.

**Recommended settings:**

| Setting | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Resolution | 1080p (1920×1080) or 4K if you have it |
| Bitrate | 8–12 Mbps (1080p) or 35–45 Mbps (4K) |
| Codec | H.264 (most compatible) or H.265 (more efficient) |
| Frame Rate | Match source (30 or 60 fps) |
| Audio | AAC, 192 kbps minimum |

**Why 8–12 Mbps for 1080p?**
- Below 8 Mbps: YouTube's compression will make it look worse
- Above 12 Mbps: Diminishing returns, larger file for no visible benefit

### TikTok Compression Settings

TikTok aggressively compresses videos, so you want to upload a file that's optimized for mobile viewing.

**Recommended settings:**

| Setting | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Resolution | 1080×1920 (vertical) |
| Bitrate | 4–6 Mbps |
| Codec | H.264 |
| Frame Rate | 30 fps (60 fps is unnecessary for most content) |
| Audio | AAC, 128 kbps |

**Why 4–6 Mbps?**
- TikTok displays on phone screens, which are small
- Higher bitrate won't be noticeable on a 6-inch screen
- Lower bitrate means faster upload and processing

### Instagram Compression Settings

Instagram compresses both Reels and Feed videos. Aim for a balance of quality and file size.

**Recommended settings:**

| Setting | Reels (9:16) | Feed (1:1 or 4:5) |
|---------|--------------|-------------------|
| Resolution | 1080×1920 | 1080×1080 or 1080×1350 |
| Bitrate | 4–6 Mbps | 4–6 Mbps |
| Codec | H.264 | H.264 |
| Frame Rate | 30 fps | 30 fps |
| Audio | AAC, 128 kbps | AAC, 128 kbps |

### LinkedIn Compression Settings

LinkedIn is a professional platform. Videos should be polished but don't need 4K quality.

**Recommended settings:**

| Setting | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Resolution | 1080p (1920×1080) or 720p (1280×720) |
| Bitrate | 4–8 Mbps |
| Codec | H.264 |
| Frame Rate | 30 fps |
| Audio | AAC, 128 kbps |

### Email and Messaging

For sending videos via email, WhatsApp, or other messaging apps, keep files under 25 MB.

**Recommended settings:**

| Setting | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Resolution | 480p (854×480) or 720p (1280×720) |
| Bitrate | 2–4 Mbps |
| Codec | H.264 |
| Frame Rate | 30 fps |
| Audio | AAC, 96 kbps |

## Advanced Compression Tips

### Tip 1: Use H.265 When Possible

H.265 (HEVC) is roughly 50% more efficient than H.264. A video that's 100 MB with H.264 might be 50 MB with H.265 – with identical quality.

**Caveat:** H.265 isn't supported by all devices and platforms. Check compatibility before using.

### Tip 2: Reduce Frame Rate for Talking Head Videos

If your video is mostly a person talking (no fast action), 30 fps is plenty. Reducing from 60 fps to 30 fps cuts file size by roughly 40% with no visible difference for this content type.

### Tip 3: Use Variable Bitrate (VBR) Instead of Constant (CBR)

VBR uses more data for complex scenes and less for simple ones. This results in better quality at the same file size. Most modern encoders default to VBR.

### Tip 4: Cut Before You Compress

Compression can't fix a video that's too long. Use the **[Video Cutter](/tools/video-cutter)** to trim unnecessary content before compressing. Shorter video = smaller file.

### Tip 5: Crop to the Right Aspect Ratio First

If you're compressing for TikTok (vertical) but your source is horizontal, crop first with the **[Video Cropper](/tools/video-cropper)**. This removes the side areas and reduces the amount of information to compress.

## Privacy: Why Local Compression Matters

When you use cloud-based video compressors, your content is uploaded to a third-party server. This creates significant risks:

**1. Content Theft**
Your unreleased video could be accessed by hackers or unscrupulous employees. For content creators, this means your exclusive content could leak.

**2. AI Training**
Some services use uploaded videos to train their compression algorithms. Your content becomes part of their model without your consent.

**3. Metadata Exposure**
Video files contain extensive metadata: camera model, serial number, GPS coordinates, software used, even the date and time of recording. Cloud tools can access all of this.

**4. Data Retention**
Many services keep copies of your files indefinitely. Even after you "delete" them, they may exist on backup servers.
