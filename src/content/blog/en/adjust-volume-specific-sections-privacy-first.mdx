## Why Section-Specific Volume Adjustment Matters

### The Uneven Audio Problem

Most amateur recordings suffer from uneven volume. Different speakers, varying microphone distances, and inconsistent recording levels all contribute to audio where some parts are too loud and others are too quiet.

**Common causes of uneven audio:**

| Cause | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Multiple speakers at different distances | Host loud, guest quiet |
| Moving closer/farther from mic | Volume changes throughout |
| Different recording sessions combined | Inconsistent levels between segments |
| Background music too loud | Voice drowned out |
| Sudden loud sounds (laughter, claps) | Peaks that clip |

### Why Simple Boosting Doesn't Work

If you boost the entire file to make quiet sections audible, the loud sections will clip (distort). If you lower the volume to prevent clipping, the quiet sections become even quieter. Section-specific adjustment solves this by treating each section differently.

### The Professional Standard

Professional audio has consistent volume throughout. Listeners shouldn't need to adjust their volume while listening. Section-specific adjustment helps achieve this consistency.

Use the **[Audio Cutter](/tools/audio-cutter)** to divide your audio into sections based on volume:

**How to identify sections:**
- Listen for volume changes

- Identify sections that need boosting vs. sections that need lowering
- Look for natural break points (pauses, topic changes)

**Split into categories:**
- Sections that are too quiet (need boosting)
- Sections that are at correct volume (no change)

**For loud sections:**

3. Add a smaller boost (+1 to +3 dB) or none at all.
4. Download.

**For sections at correct volume:**
- No processing needed.

After adjusting individual sections, apply light compression to the entire file to smooth out remaining differences:

1. Open the **[Audio Compressor](/tools/audio-compressor)**.

3. Apply level 2–3 compression.
4. Download.

### Step 4: Merge the Sections Back Together

1. Upload all sections in order.
2. Merge.
3. Download.

### Step 5: Final Polish

Apply any final processing:
- Compression (if not done in Step 3)
- Volume boost (if the overall file is still too quiet)

## Common Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Host Loud, Guest Quiet

**The problem:** In an interview, the host speaks at -14 LUFS while the guest speaks at -24 LUFS. The volume difference is jarring.

**The solution:**

1. Split the interview into host sections and guest sections.
2. Boost guest sections by +8 dB.
3. Leave host sections unchanged (or boost slightly by +2 dB).
4. Merge everything back together.
5. Apply light compression (level 3) to smooth remaining differences.

**Result:** Both speakers are now at similar volumes. Listeners don't need to adjust their volume.

### Scenario 2: Loud Intro Music, Quiet Voiceover

**The problem:** Your podcast intro music is at -8 LUFS (too loud) while your voiceover is at -20 LUFS (too quiet).

**The solution:**

1. Split the intro music from the voiceover.
2. Reduce the music volume (or use a quieter music source).
3. Boost the voiceover by +6 dB.
4. Merge music (quieter) with voiceover (louder).
5. The music should be 10–14 dB below the voice.

**Result:** A balanced intro where the voice is clearly audible over the music.

### Scenario 3: Sudden Loud Laughter

**The problem:** During a conversation, someone laughed very close to the microphone, creating a loud peak that nearly clips.

**The solution:**

1. Split the section containing the laughter.
2. Apply compression to just that section (level 4–5).
3. Boost the rest of the audio normally (+6 dB).
4. Merge all sections back together.

**Result:** The laughter is controlled, and the overall volume is consistent.

### Scenario 4: Meeting with Distant Participants

**The problem:** A meeting recording where in-person participants are loud and remote participants (via speakerphone) are quiet.

**The solution:**

1. Split the recording into in-person sections and remote sections.
2. Boost remote sections by +9 to +12 dB.
3. Leave in-person sections at original volume.
4. Merge.
5. Apply compression (level 3–4) for consistency.

**Result:** All participants are audible, regardless of their distance from the microphone.

## Advanced Tips

### Tip 1: Use Compression First, Then Boost

For sections with internal volume variation, compress first to even out the dynamics, then boost to the desired level. This prevents clipping within the section.

### Tip 2: Match Loudness Levels

Aim for consistent loudness across all sections:

| Content Type | Target Loudness |
|--------------|-----------------|
| Podcast | -16 LUFS |
| YouTube | -14 LUFS |
| Audiobook | -18 to -23 LUFS |
| General speech | -16 LUFS |

### Tip 3: Leave Natural Dynamics

Don't over-compress. Some variation is natural and desirable. The goal is to prevent extremes, not eliminate all dynamics.

### Tip 4: Process in the Right Order

Always:
1. Split
2. Adjust individual sections
3. Merge
4. Compress (if needed)
5. Boost (if needed)
6. Convert
7. Clean metadata

### Tip 5: Keep the Original

Always keep the original unprocessed file. You may need to re-split with different boundaries if the first attempt doesn't sound right.
