## Common Audio Problems and Their Fixes

Before diving into the workflow, let's understand the most common issues:

| Problem | Sound | Cause | Primary Fix |
|---------|-------|-------|-------------|
| **Background Hum** | Low "mmmm" sound | Electrical interference, fans | Compression + Trim |
| **Hiss** | High "ssss" sound | Cheap microphone, high gain | Compression |
| **Clicks and Pops** | Sharp, sudden sounds | Mouth noises, mouse clicks, keyboard | Trim (manual removal) |
| **Distortion** | Fuzzy, crackly sound | Recording too hot (clipping) | Prevention (can't fully fix) |
| **Room Echo** | Hollow, distant sound | Hard surfaces, far from mic | Prevention (record better) |
| **Plosives** | "P" and "B" pops | Speaking too close to mic | Trim + Prevention |
| **Inconsistent Volume** | Loud/quiet variations | Moving around, multiple speakers | Compression + Boost |

**Key insight:** Some problems can be fixed after recording; others are best prevented. Focus on fixing what you can and preventing the rest.

## The Complete Audio Cleanup Workflow

Here's the step-by-step process for cleaning up any audio recording:

### Step 1: Trim Unwanted Sections

The first step is removing sections that don't belong: long silences, false starts, coughs, and interruptions.

**How to do it:**

1. Open the **[Audio Cutter](/tools/audio-cutter)**.
2. Upload your audio file (it stays on your device).
3. Use the waveform to identify:
   - Flat areas (silence)
   - Sudden spikes (clicks, coughs)
   - Sections you know you want removed
4. Set start and end points to keep only the good content.
5. Download the trimmed file.

**What to remove:**
- Silence longer than 2 seconds
- False starts ("I think... I think...")
- Coughs and throat clears
- Background interruptions (door slams, phone rings)
- Off-topic tangents

**Result:** A tighter recording with fewer distractions.

### Step 2: Reduce Background Noise with Compression

Compression reduces the dynamic range, making quiet background noise less noticeable relative to your voice.

**How to do it:**

1. Open the **[Audio Compressor](/tools/audio-compressor)**.
2. Upload your trimmed file.
3. Choose compression level:
   - **Level 3** – Light noise (home studio)
   - **Level 4** – Moderate noise (office, fan)
   - **Level 5** – Heavy noise (outdoor, busy room)
4. Download the compressed file.

**What compression does:**
- Makes quiet noise less audible
- Makes voice more consistent
- Creates a "tighter" sound

### Step 3: Boost Volume to Professional Levels

After compression, your audio is cleaner but may be too quiet. Boost to reach -16 LUFS.

**How to do it:**

1. Open the **[Volume Booster](/tools/volume-booster)**.
2. Upload your compressed file.
3. Add +6 dB to start.
4. Listen to a sample. If still quiet, add more (up to +12 dB).
5. Be careful not to clip. Peaks should stay below -1 dB.
6. Download the boosted file.

### Step 4: Convert to the Right Format

Output your cleaned audio in the format required by your platform.

**How to do it:**

1. Open the **[Audio Converter](/tools/audio-converter)**.
2. Upload your processed file.
3. Choose output format:
   - **MP3 (128 kbps)** – Podcasts, general distribution
   - **MP3 (192 kbps)** – YouTube, higher quality
   - **M4A** – Apple platforms
   - **WAV** – Further editing, archiving
4. Download.

### Step 5: Clean Metadata for Privacy

Always strip identifying information before sharing.

**How to do it:**

Toggle **"Clean File Privacy Before Download"** on every tool.

**What this removes:**
- Author name
- Device model
- GPS coordinates
- Software information
- Date/time stamps

## Advanced Cleanup Techniques

### Technique 1: Manual Click Removal

For individual clicks and pops:

1. Use the Audio Cutter to isolate the click.
2. Remove just that tiny section (usually 0.1–0.3 seconds).
3. The surrounding audio will join seamlessly.

### Technique 2: Layered Compression

For stubborn noise:

1. Apply light compression (level 2–3).
2. Download.
3. Apply again (level 2–3).
4. This "double compression" can reduce noise more effectively than a single aggressive pass.

If the recording sounds sluggish, a slight speed increase (1.05x–1.1x) can improve perceived quality:

## What Cannot Be Fixed (And How to Prevent It)

Some problems are difficult or impossible to fix after recording. Here's what to watch for:

### Distortion (Clipping)

If the recording is distorted (recorded too hot), it cannot be fully fixed. The audio data is corrupted.

**Prevention:** Set gain so peaks hit -12 to -6 dB. Leave headroom.

### Severe Room Echo

Mild echo can be reduced with compression, but severe echo from a large, empty room is hard to fix.

**Prevention:** Record in a soft room (carpets, curtains, furniture). Or use blankets around your recording area.

### Missing Audio

If a section was never recorded (battery died, forgot to hit record), it cannot be recovered.

**Prevention:** Always check recording status before starting. Use backup recording devices for important content.
