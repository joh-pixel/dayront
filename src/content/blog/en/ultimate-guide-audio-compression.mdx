# The Ultimate Guide to Audio Compression: Reduce File Size Without Losing Quality

The word “compression” has two meanings in audio: **dynamic range compression** and **data compression**. This guide covers both, with a focus on how to use Dayront’s Compressor tool to improve your audio while reducing file size.

## Dynamic Range Compression vs Data Compression

### Dynamic Range Compression
This evens out the volume levels – it makes quiet sounds louder and loud sounds quieter. It’s used in music production, podcasts, and broadcasting to create a consistent listening volume.

### Data Compression
This reduces the file size. Examples: encoding a WAV file to MP3, or reducing the bitrate of an existing MP3.

Dayront’s **Audio Compressor** currently focuses on data compression, but we have plans for a dynamic range compressor too!

## Why Compress Audio?

- **Save storage space** – especially on phones and portable devices.
- **Faster uploads/downloads** – smaller files transfer quicker.
- **Streaming friendly** – lower bitrates use less bandwidth.
- **Better battery life** – smaller files require less processing.

## How Dayront’s Compressor Works

Our Compressor uses FFmpeg’s `libmp3lame` encoder with a variable quality setting (0‑9, where 0 is best quality and 9 is smallest size). The default is 3, which balances quality and size.

## When Not to Compress

- **Archival copies** – always keep a lossless master.
- **Production files** – compress only the final deliverable, not the working files.
- **Already highly compressed files** – compressing an MP3 further degrades quality.
