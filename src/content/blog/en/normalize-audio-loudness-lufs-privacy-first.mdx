## What Is LUFS and Why Does It Matter?

### LUFS Explained

**LUFS** stands for **Loudness Units relative to Full Scale**. It's a standard measurement of perceived audio loudness that takes into account how human ears respond to different frequencies and volumes. Unlike peak level (which only measures the highest moment), LUFS represents the average loudness over time.

Think of it this way: peak level tells you the loudest a sound gets. LUFS tells you how loud it *feels* over the whole recording.

### The Standards

Different platforms target different LUFS levels:

| Platform | Target LUFS | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| Apple Podcasts | -16 LUFS | Standard for spoken word |
| Spotify | -14 LUFS (music) / -16 LUFS (podcasts) | Varies by content type |
| YouTube | -14 LUFS | For all video content |
| Amazon Music | -14 LUFS | Music and podcasts |
| Audible (audiobooks) | -18 LUFS | Slightly quieter for narration |

**Why the variation?** Music is typically more compressed and louder than speech. Speech needs more dynamic range to remain intelligible.

### What Happens If You Don't Normalize?

- **Too quiet:** Listeners turn up volume, then ads or other content blast them.
- **Too loud:** Platform compresses or limits your audio, causing artifacts.
- **Inconsistent:** Different episodes at different levels frustrate loyal listeners.

Normalizing to the right LUFS ensures your content plays at the same perceived volume as everything else on the platform.

## Why This Method Works

Compression reduces the difference between your loudest and quietest moments. This raises the *average* loudness without increasing the *peak* level. Then boosting adds uniform gain to bring the entire track up to the target. Together, they increase perceived loudness without causing clipping.

### Example

**Original recording:**
- Quiet passages: -25 dBFS
- Loud passages: -6 dBFS
- Average (LUFS): approximately -23 LUFS (too quiet)

**After compression (level 4):**
- Quiet passages: -20 dBFS
- Loud passages: -10 dBFS
- Average: approximately -19 LUFS

**After +6 dB boost:**
- Quiet passages: -14 dBFS
- Loud passages: -4 dBFS
- Average: approximately -16 LUFS (perfect)

## Platform-Specific Tips

### Apple Podcasts

Target **-16 LUFS**. Apple will apply its own loudness normalization if you're off, but it's better to submit at the correct level to avoid extra processing.

### Spotify

Spotify normalizes all audio to -14 LUFS (music) or -16 LUFS (podcasts). If you upload at -16 LUFS, you're already compliant.

### YouTube

YouTube targets -14 LUFS, but for podcast-style videos, -16 LUFS is still fine. Use the same workflow but consider a slightly higher boost (+7 or +8 dB) to reach -14.

### Audiobooks (Audible)

Audible requires -18 LUFS to -23 LUFS RMS. This means slightly quieter and more dynamic than podcasts. Use lighter compression (level 2) and less boost (+3 to +4 dB).

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Boosting Without Compressing First

If you boost a raw, uncompressed recording, the loud peaks will clip while the quiet parts remain too quiet. Always compress first, then boost.

### 2. Over-compressing

Too much compression makes audio sound flat and lifeless. For spoken word, stick to levels 3–5. If you hear pumping or breathing artifacts, reduce compression.

### 3. Ignoring Peak Levels

While LUFS is about average loudness, peaks still matter. Keep peaks below -1 dB to avoid clipping. After boosting, if you see red in a meter, you've gone too far.

### 4. Applying One Setting to All Content

Music, speech, and sound effects have different loudness requirements. Adjust compression and boost based on content type.

### 5. Not Cleaning Metadata

Always toggle "Clean File Privacy" before downloading. This removes device info, GPS, and author names from your audio file.
